# User guide

Korinnia keeps your family's history in one place: a family tree that grows from the oldest
ancestors upwards, life stories, photos, documents and the sources they come from. This guide
walks through everything you can do. Buttons and menu names are written **like this**.

## Signing in and your profile

- Sign in with your email and password. There is no public sign-up: an administrator creates
  your account or sends you an invitation link. Forgot your password? Ask an administrator for
  a reset link.
- A sign-in lasts 8 hours (an administrator can change this). Two minutes before the end a
  note offers **Stay signed in**; if you miss it, a window asks for your password again and
  **Refresh session** brings you back to the same page.
- Just looking around? **Try the demo** on the sign-in page (or sign in with
  `demo@example.com` / `demo@example.com`, if the site offers it) gives you your own copy of
  the demo family for 30 minutes. Nobody else sees your changes, and the copy is deleted when
  the demo ends; **Start a new demo** gives you a fresh one. Sharing and two-step
  verification are not available in the demo.
- **Theme** (light, dark, archive / sepia, Night (glass: a soft blurred colour background behind
  frosted panels), or follow your device) and **Language** (English,
  Українська, Polski) are in the top bar. Your language is remembered on every device.
- Your name at the top right opens **Your profile**: display name, interface language,
  **Change password** (this signs you out on your other devices) and **Two-step verification**.
  Two-step verification asks for a code from an authenticator app after the password; keep the
  recovery codes somewhere safe. Recommended for administrators.
- After signing in you may see **What's new** (recent changes) or the welcome guide. Both can be
  turned off with the checkbox in the window, and both are always in the page footer
  (**Getting started**, **What's new**).

## Your family trees

**Family trees** lists every tree you can open, with portraits, the number of people and the
years covered. Your role in each tree is shown on its card.

- Everyone gets their own **Demo** family (66 invented people over seven generations) to explore
  and change freely; it can be deleted like any tree.
- Start your own family with **New tree**, or **Import GEDCOM** (a tree made from a file — see
  _Import and export_). You are the administrator of every tree you start: you decide who sees
  it and who may edit it. Nobody else sees your tree unless you share it (site administrators
  can see every tree to help with problems). On some sites only administrators create trees;
  then the buttons are missing and an administrator makes the tree for you.
- On the cards of trees you administer: **Share**, **Rename** and **Delete**. A deleted tree
  disappears for everyone, but nothing is destroyed: a site administrator can restore it.

## Exploring the tree

The tree is drawn the way it grows: the oldest generation stands on the ground with roots and a
trunk, every generation above it in its own row, couples side by side, branches leading up to
their children. Labels on the left name each generation and its decade.

- **Move** by dragging the empty background; **zoom** with the mouse wheel, a pinch, or the
  **+ / −** buttons; **Fit to screen** shows everything. The overview map in the corner shows
  where you are. Cards show the portrait, name and life years; only very large trees seen as a whole show just the names.
- **Whole tree** shows everyone. **Focus** shows only a few generations around one person
  (choose how many with **Generations**); small ▲ / ▼ marks mean there are more relatives
  beyond. Trees with more than 150 people open in focus mode.
- Pointing at a card highlights that person's line: ancestors, descendants and partners. Hover
  a card for a short preview.
- Couples are joined by a link: interlocked rings and the wedding year for a marriage, a dashed
  line with a heart for partners who are not married, struck-through rings after a divorce or
  separation. Point at the rings for the date and place.
- **Find a person, place or year…** finds names in any spelling — Cyrillic or Latin, married or
  maiden names — places (also by their historical names) and years. The result is centred and
  opened.
- **Style** changes the drawing: **Simple**, **Oak** (a leafy crown) or **Spring** (leaves and
  blossoms). It is remembered in your browser.
- **Download** in the top-right corner of the tree: **PNG** (an image), **PDF** (one page) or
  **GEDCOM** (the data, for other genealogy programs).

### A person's details

Click a card to open the side panel: portrait, life years, facts, biography, family and photos.
**Focus here** centres the focus view on this person, **Open full page** shows everything:

- all names (birth, married, nicknames, other scripts), birth, death and burial (with the
  cemetery and a map link), occupation, education, religion, nationality;
- the biography and **Notes for descendants**;
- a **Life timeline** and a life bar with the events of the person's life;
- **Family** — parents, partners, children and siblings, each a link;
- photos, documents, recordings and videos; sources; stories; comments and research questions;
- **How are we related?**, **On the map** and **Timeline** links for this person.

## Building the tree (editors)

Editors and administrators switch the tree from **View** to **Builder**.

- **Add person** adds someone without links; in an empty tree, **Add the first person** — start
  with yourself, your parents or the oldest ancestor you know.
- Every card gets **+** buttons: below for a parent, above for a child, on the right for a
  partner, on the left for a sibling. The new person is created and linked in one step.
- **Edit** (on the card's panel or person page) opens the editor with tabs:
  - **Details** — names (as many as needed, each with its script and type: birth, married,
    nickname…; one is the main name), gender, living or deceased, birth and death with place,
    occupation, education, religion, nationality, biography (Markdown, with a preview; drafts are
    kept in your browser until saved), notes for descendants, research status;
  - **Events** — education, military service, emigration, residence, awards and more, each with
    a date, place, description and the people who took part; burials with cemetery, plot and a
    map pin;
  - **Family** — change the kind of a parent link (biological, adoptive, step, foster), unlink,
    link existing people, edit a partnership's dates and how it ended;
  - **Media**, **Sources** (cite a source for any fact) and **History** (every earlier version,
    with **Restore this version**).
- **Dates** can be as exact as you know them: a year, a month, a full date, _about_, _before_,
  _after_ or _between_ two dates. The original wording ("at Easter 1888") can be kept as well.
  The app warns about impossible dates (a child born before a parent, a death before a birth).
- **Places** are typed with autocomplete (current and historical names, any script); a new place
  can get historical names ("Stanislau → Stanisławów → Ivano-Frankivsk") and a map pin.
- **Drag** a card to arrange the tree: the whole branch (partners and descendants) moves with
  it; hold **Alt** to move a single card. Cards never overlap — others make room.
  **Reset layout** returns to the automatic arrangement.
- **Delete** (in the side panel in Builder, or in the editor) shows what will be removed with
  the person; everything goes to the **Trash**, from where it can be restored. Nothing is ever
  erased for good.

### Places (editors)

- On a place's page, **Edit place** changes its name, region, country, map pin and former
  names (each with the years it was used).
- **Merge into another place** is for duplicates, such as the same village entered twice:
  choose the place to keep. Events, marriages and photos move there, and the merged place's
  names are kept as its former names. The merged place goes to the **Trash**; restoring it
  from there undoes the merge.

## Photos, documents and recordings

- On a person's page or in the editor's **Media** tab, drag files in or **Choose files**:
  photos (also HEIC), document scans, PDFs, audio and video. Tick _These are document scans_
  for documents. Large files are fine.
- Originals are stored exactly as uploaded, with a checksum; previews and thumbnails are made
  automatically. The same file is not stored twice in a tree — you can link the existing one.
- **Tag people**: draw a rectangle around a face and choose who it is. **Use as portrait**: crop
  a square for the picture shown on the tree (the original is never changed).
- When you add photos in the **Media** tab, a short dialog asks for each one's **description**
  right away (title, date and place are optional). Under **Who else is in the photo**, choose the
  other people from the list — family shows up at once, anyone else by name; the photo then
  appears on their pages too. **Later** skips all of this; **Edit** and **Tag people** fill it in
  at any time. The description is shown above the photo in the family album.
- Each item can have a title, description, date and place, and comments. **Download original**
  gives the file as uploaded.

## Family album

- Every photo of the tree in one place, for everyone who can see the tree (also visitors with a
  share link, without photos of living people — see **Privacy of living people**). Documents, scans, PDFs and videos are not in it; a photo appears once its preview
  is ready.
- On the tree page, the **Family album** button next to the tree's name (with the number of
  photos) opens the album in a window over the tree; ✕, Esc or a click outside closes it.
  **Show all** opens the album page, which is also in the row of tree sections.
- Above each photo: its title in bold, the description (**more** for a long one), then date ·
  place. Below it, the people in the photo: click a name for **Only photos of this person**,
  **Show on the tree** (closes the album and centres the tree on them) or **Open their page**.
  Click the photo to see it full screen, where you can swipe too.
- Browse by swiping, with the ‹ › buttons on the photo, the ← → keys or the thumbnails. **Play slideshow** turns to
  the next photo every few seconds; it pauses while the pointer or the keyboard is on the photos
  and while the tab is in the background, and is not offered when your device asks for reduced
  motion.
- The album page shows a large carousel or a **Grid**, with filters by who is in the photo
  (several people at once) and by decade, a search in descriptions, and the order: by photo date
  (undated photos last) or newest uploads first.

## Sources and citations

- **Sources** (in the tree's menu) is the tree's library: civil and church records, archive
  documents, censuses, newspapers, letters, oral history, books, websites — with the archive,
  reference number, link and scans attached.
- A citation says "this fact comes from that source": cite a whole person, a name, an event, a
  parent link, a partnership or a photo, with the page and how reliable it is (**Primary
  source**, **Secondary source**, **Family tradition**, **Unverified**).

## Stories, comments and research

- **Stories** are memories and family stories in Markdown, with a narrator, a date, the people
  they are about and photos. Contributors write them and **Submit for review**; editors
  **Publish** them or **Return to author** with a comment.
- **Comments** on people, stories and photos are for memories and notes.
- **Research questions** on a person's page keep track of what is still unknown ("Where was he
  during 1941–1945?") until they are **Marked resolved** with the answer.
- **Data quality** lists the research to-do: date conflicts, people without a birth date,
  without sources, without a photo, and open questions.

## Discovering the family

The links under the tree's title:

- **Timeline** — every dated event and marriage by decade, with filters by type, person and
  years, and optional historical context (the world wars, the Holodomor, Chornobyl,
  independence…).
- **Places** — every place of the tree, most events first; search finds former names too. A
  place's page shows its current and former names (with the years each was used), a small map
  with the places nearby, the people born, married, living, dying or buried there grouped by
  event, photos taken there and stories that mention it. **Show on the family map** opens the
  map at that place. Place names on person pages, in event lists, on the map and in the album
  link to these pages.
- **Map** — where the family was born, lived, married, moved, died and is buried; filters by
  event type and person, with a person's life path drawn in order.
- **On this day** — birthdays, wedding anniversaries and memorial days for a date or the coming
  week, month or three months.
- **Relationship** — choose two people to get the exact kinship term ("first cousin once
  removed", in Ukrainian "троюрідний брат", "свекор"…), the closest common ancestors and the
  path between them.
- **Statistics** — people, generations, the period covered, the oldest ancestor, the longest
  life, average lifespan, surnames (all spellings together), first names, and births and deaths
  by decade.

### Birthdays in your calendar

- **Subscribe in your calendar** on the **On this day** page gives you a private link for
  Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, Outlook or any app that subscribes to calendars by URL. The
  tree's birthdays, memorial days and wedding anniversaries then appear there every year, and
  the calendar picks up changes by itself (usually within a day).
- Choose the language of the events, then **Create link**: copy it, or press **Open in your
  calendar app**. The link is shown only once; if you lose it, **Get a new link** (the old one
  stops working).
- Only dates known to the day are included. The calendar shows what you may see in the tree:
  if living people's details are hidden from you, so are their birthdays.
- The link works only while you are a member of the tree. **Revoke** turns it off at once.
  Do not share it: anyone who has it can see these dates.

## Working together

Every tree has members, each with a role:

| Role              | Can                                                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Viewer**        | see the tree, people, photos, sources and published stories                                                |
| **Contributor**   | also suggest edits and biographies, write stories (reviewed by an editor), comment, add research questions |
| **Editor**        | also edit people, families, media and sources directly; review suggestions and stories                     |
| **Administrator** | also manage members, share links and the tree itself                                                       |

- **Share** (on the tree card or in the Builder toolbar) adds an existing user by email with the
  access you choose. If nobody has that email, the app says so.
- **Anyone with the link can view** (in the same dialog) creates a link for relatives without an
  account: they see the tree read-only, without signing in. Give it a name and an expiry, and
  revoke it when it is no longer needed.
- **Members** (in the tree's menu, administrators) lists everyone with access: change roles,
  remove people, or create an invitation link for someone who has no account yet (valid for
  7 days, works once).
- Contributors use **Suggest an edit** on a person's page; editors see a badge on
  **Moderation** and **Approve and apply** or **Reject** each suggestion.
- **History** (administrators) is the tree's change log: who changed what and when.

### Privacy of living people

- On the **Members** page, **Privacy of living people** decides who sees only the basics of
  living people — their name, gender, birth year and family links:
  - **Nobody** — everyone who can open the tree sees everything;
  - **Visitors with a share link** (the default) — members see everything;
  - **Viewers and visitors with a share link** — members with the viewer role see only the
    basics too.
- For them, a living person's dates, places, events, biography and facts, photos and documents,
  stories only about them, comments and research questions are left out, and the family album,
  map, timeline and "On this day" skip them. A 🔒 on the card and the person's page says so.
- **Show portraits of living people** keeps their portrait on cards; their photos stay hidden.
- Contributors, editors and administrators always see everything.

## Import and export

- **Import GEDCOM** brings in a tree from MyHeritage, Ancestry, Gramps, FamilySearch, Geni and
  other programs (GEDCOM 5.5 / 5.5.1 files, any common encoding). In an existing tree it is in
  the Builder toolbar; on the tree list it makes a new tree from the file.
  1. Choose the `.ged` file. It is read first, nothing is saved yet.
  2. Check the preview: how many people, families, events, places and sources; what cannot be
     imported (photos are not part of a GEDCOM file).
  3. People who look like someone already in the tree are listed — tick **Same person** to keep
     the existing person instead of creating a copy.
  4. **Import**. Anything the tree cannot accept (a third biological parent, an impossible date)
     is skipped and listed; the rest is saved.
  5. Changed your mind? **Undo import** moves everything it created to the trash.
- **GEDCOM** in the tree's Download box exports the whole tree for other programs — optionally
  without the details of living people. Photos are not included.
- **PNG** and **PDF** download a picture of the whole tree in the current style and theme.

## For administrators

**Administration** (site administrators only):

- **Users** — create accounts, reset passwords (a link to send), turn off two-step verification
  for someone who lost their phone, deactivate or delete accounts, grant administrator rights,
  **Stop creating trees** / **Allow creating trees** for one person.
- **Trees** — every tree on the site: delete, or restore deleted ones.
- **Invitations** — invitation links to the site or to a tree.
- **Change history** — every change on the site, with filters.
- **System status** — the server, database and file storage, the running version, and **File
  integrity**: every week (or **Check now**) all stored originals are read back and compared with
  their checksums; missing or changed files are listed.

## Tips

- Not sure of a date? Enter what you know — a year, _about_, or _between_ — and keep the
  original wording. A vague date is better than none.
- Write names as they were: Cyrillic, Latin, maiden and married names are all searchable, and
  search finds "Kovalenko" when you type "Коваленко" and the other way round.
- Share links show living people with only the basics (see **Privacy of living people**); still
  give them an expiry and send them only to people you trust.
- Nothing you delete is lost: people, events, photos, sources and stories go to the **Trash**,
  and every change can be undone from **History**.
